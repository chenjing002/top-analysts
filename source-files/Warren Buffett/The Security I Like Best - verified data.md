## Western Insurance Securities — page 2

Buffett discusses the operating subsidiaries Western Casualty & Surety and Western Fire, premium growth, capital structure, underwriting performance, and investment assets. The article states that Western Insurance Securities owned 92% of Western Casualty & Surety, which owned 99.95% of Western Fire. Its 1952 consolidated admitted assets were $29,590,142. Buffett describes the common stock as trading at about $40.

## Oil and Gas Property Management, Inc. — page 5

Buffett describes oil and gas reserves, obligations attached to the properties, and the resulting sensitivity of common stock value to commodity prices. The article gives 380,000 common shares with an aggregate market value of about $5 million and describes roughly $65 million of obligations against an estimated $150 million gross value of oil and gas. The reserve table is based on the company's 1955 annual report.

| Net reserves for stock and debentures | Estimated gross value of net reserves | Valuation rate printed in article |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 26,600,000 barrels of oil | $73,150,000 | $2.75 per barrel |
| 199,800,000 MCF of gas | $17,982,000 | $0.09 per MCF |

The article states that an oil price increase of $0.15 per barrel, or a gas price increase of $0.01 per MCF, would equal the then-current total market value of the common stock. It compares O & G with Producing Properties, Inc. and mentions a half interest in Yemen Development Corp.
