# Observer — 605499.SH 2026-11-23-02 round 01
**Reviewed:** rounds/round-01.json · experts: stephen-penman, li-lu, warren-buffett, jeff-bezos · 2026-11-23 (independent review; numbers recomputed from read-only bme.duckdb, report_type 1, quarters de-cumulated; statement numbers below are 1-based positions in `statements[]`)

## Verdict
The round answers its question in a usable way: the price at 8% needs modest perpetual residual-earnings growth, the Q3 profit swing is split into an operating part and a financial part, and the pause-versus-end question is left open with dated tests. Every headline number I rechecked reproduces. The 2.8% is 2.75%, which rounds to 2.7%, a rounding slip with no effect on the argument. The weak point is the bridge between the numbers and the stated answer. The conclusion says the price "does not assume growth ends", but Buffett's 1.6% (turn 9) is below inflation, which reads as roughly no real growth. The 1.6% is also internally inconsistent: the cash is removed from price and book value, but its interest stays in earnings. The dealer-prepayment test is also soft, because the year-end threshold ignores the seasonal build. Confidence "low" is earned and arguably slightly too low for the narrow claim "price does not need zero nominal growth at r ≤ 8%". It is not earned for the "leans to a pause" half, which rests on margins that cannot tell a pause from an end. Short turns kept the argument legible. They cost the round the base-effect, seasonality and no-growth-value points, and turns 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 break the three-figure cap.

## Patterns worth keeping
- A claim changed by evidence — Buffett t7 → Penman t8 (and Li Lu t11). Buffett showed that gross margin and selling ratio move in opposite directions, so the "marketing cut" disappears when the two are netted (Q2 32.5%, Q3 32.3% vs 30.9%, 29.9%). Penman conceded explicitly, and the bridge was reframed. This is the clearest case of one expert's number moving another's.
- A challenge that exposed a hidden assumption — Buffett t9 on charging 8% on the new cash. It shows the equity-basis curve treats raised cash as an operating asset. The direction is right (excess cash earning less than 8% lowers the growth the price needs; the size is disputed in the weaknesses), and it is a real driver of the 5.0% → 2.8% fall, because year-end book value was 94 and is now 186.
- A reconciling derivation — Penman t6 splits the Q3 pretax change into a +1.7 part and a −2.6 part. It closes to −0.99 (gross profit +0.49, selling +1.06, admin −0.45, net interest +0.55, other financial cost −1.13, fair value and investment −1.46). Given as a bridge it can be checked line by line.
- Role cards that earned their place — Li Lu's "what would change my mind" with dated, numeric tests (t5, t11). Bezos's turn from prepayments to capex ending in a Q4 test (t10). Both end on something testable, as the Analyze skill requires.
- An honest open label — Li Lu t11 and the conclusion put "3.55 other financial cost" and "99 亿 use" in open questions instead of guessing a cause.

## Weaknesses
- [severity: medium] Headline answer and Buffett's number point in different directions — conclusion.answer vs Buffett t9 and Li Lu t11 — "Does not assume growth ends" is carried by 2.8%, yet the same round shows 1.6% (below inflation) if the cash is excluded. Li Lu's concession is "at 8% or less", and at 10% the price needs 5.2% (t8). The conclusion should say the answer depends on r and on the cash treatment: roughly no real growth at the low end, and fairly demanding growth at 10%. The workpaper's no-growth value (600 at 8%, 480 at 10%, against 834) is a cleaner way to put the point and never reached the chat.
- [severity: medium] The 1.6% is not like-for-like — Buffett t9 and the workpaper — P and B drop 98.85, but TTM earnings (47.99) keep the cash's interest income. Removing about 3% pretax on 98.85 (about 2.6 after tax) from earnings gives about 1.9–2.0% (my recompute), not 1.6%. "Less than inflation" is therefore not established. "Roughly 3% on idle cash" is also not in the evidence list. Quarterly interest income was 1.00 in Q3 on about 141 of cash, which gives about 2.8% annualised, so it is plausible. But "idle" is an assumption: cash balance fell 149.5 → 141.5 since March, capex continues, and dividends of 34.6 were paid in nine months. Buffett half-flags this ("depends on what the company does") but the 1.6% is still presented as a number.
- [severity: medium] Earnings base not cleaned — Penman t8 — Residual earnings are charged on reported TTM parent profit (47.99). That includes about 3.7 pretax of fair-value and investment gains (mostly Q2: 2.58 + 0.33). Penman's card says to separate core from unusual items, and Penman himself calls the −1.5 fall in these gains a drag in t6. Stripping about 3.2 after tax lifts the 8% implied growth to about 3.2%, so 2.8% understates what the price needs. The 8% itself is the model default and is never justified (OBSERVER §1 confidence inflation; AGENTS requires justifying inputs).
- [severity: medium] Penman did not do his own card's cash split, and Buffett did it for him — t8 vs the Penman card §3.1 (excess cash is financial, not operating). The curve is on an equity basis with 99 亿 of excess cash inside book value. Role cards did differentiate the experts overall, but here the blind spot materialised and only another expert caught it. Penman also never replied to Buffett's challenge (no Penman turn after t9). The disagreement is marked "narrowed" with "both numbers are shown", which leaves the main valuation number unresolved.
- [severity: medium] "Operating gain +1.7" includes financing — Penman t6 — The 0.55 net-interest line is interest on the raised cash (interest income 1.00 vs 0.49), so the operating gain is about +1.1. Calling the profit engine "sales, not profit" while counting financing income inside it conflicts with Penman's own operations versus financing split.
- [severity: medium] Dealer-prepayment test is soft and Bezos's inference is overread — Bezos t4 and t10, Li Lu t5 — "Dealers have not blinked" rests on a +43% yoy figure whose base (35.63 at September 2025) was seasonally low, against a revenue growth of 0.75%. A balance up 43% on flat sales is as consistent with a loaded channel (Li Lu's reading) as with confidence. Li Lu answered the point, which is good, but then set the year-end test at 59.7 ("holding near 60"). The Q3-to-Q4 build was +24 in 2025 (35.6 → 59.7) and +24 in 2024 (23.8 → 47.6), so a year-end 60 from 50.9 is a build of only about 9, a sharp slowdown by seasonal pattern, and an easy bar to call "held". The test as worded cannot separate the two readings. The disagreement is labelled "open", which is right.
- [severity: medium] Cash evidence selected one way — Bezos t4 and t10 — Nine-month operating cash flow +13% (35.42 vs 31.33) is real, but the single-quarter figure is Q3 10.49 vs 13.92, down 25%, with Q2 (20.40 vs 11.09) doing the lifting. "Cash is up while building slows" is true over nine months and false for the quarter at issue. His card's own checks (free cash flow per share with dilution; share count up 41%) are not run, so "cash is up" is not per share.
- [severity: medium] Mix and "same price" asserted without data — Buffett t3 and Penman t8 — Stable gross margin (45.7% vs 45.2%) shows no discounting but does not show "same price sells fewer bottles"; mix or cost shifts give the same margin. Penman then says the gain "comes from the product mix and cost side", and no product-level or cost-line data was read. The "resolved" status on the marketing disagreement (Buffett: "it moved the cost around") is a hypothesis, not a finding. It may be classification between revenue deductions and selling expense; the Q3 selling expense fall (−1.06) is real in absolute terms and nobody tested the cause.
- [severity: low] Q3-only versus nine-month — Buffett t7 — Nine-month selling ratio is up (16.0% vs 15.5%), but the Q3 gain in net margin comes mainly from selling (−1.9 points of revenue) and only about +0.5 from gross margin. "The gain is gross margin" is true over nine months (47.5% vs 45.2%) and not for Q3.
- [severity: low] Chat register — turns 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 — Word counts are fine (55–84, all under 110) and no formulas or source codes appear in `text`, with at most two evidence items. But the three-figure cap is broken: t7 has about 16 figures, t1 about 11, t6 about 10, t8 about 9, t2 about 8. Turn 7 should have been split or trimmed to the 32% pair and the nine-month pair.
- [severity: low] Concession without a referent — Buffett t7 to Li Lu — "The Q3 gain in profit is not a sign of a healthier sales engine" answers a claim Li Lu did not make in this round. Li Lu's t2 point was about the slowing trend. Penman's t2-directed challenge (one slow quarter is not evidence of a pause) also never got a reply on the base effect: 2025 yoy growth of +39%, +34% and +30% makes the two-year stack fall partly by construction.
- [severity: low] Share-count scope — Penman t1 — The 5.2 → 7.3 move is partly a ×1.30 step between Q1 and Q2 that looks like a bonus issue without cash. The round flags this as an open question, which is correct, but "market cap down 40%" (833.7 vs 1,390.5, checked) and the share count change cannot yet be read as dilution.

## Suggested next question
