### Stephen Penman

It is not from pricing. The energy drink's gross margin hit a record 54.7% in the first half, up from 50.8% for all of last year, while its sales grew only about 7%. A four-point margin jump on slow volume is the signature of cheaper inputs — the plastic and sugar it buys — not of a company raising prices. I would treat that cushion as borrowed, not earned. Before we pay for this profit I want to know what it looks like when input costs are normal, and that test is in the interim report's cost note, which we do not have locally.

### Warren Buffett

I went back to the 1986 letter for the test I trust. ## Conclusion

**Answer (low confidence):** The 2026 stall is a volume and base story, not a price war, and the record profit margin is a cost windfall that masks a mix shift toward thinner-margin drinks. The flagship energy drink still grew about 7% in the first half, but the electrolyte second curve braked from 118% growth last year to 12%, and tea — at roughly 24 cents of gross profit per yuan against 55 for energy — now supplies more than half the sales gain. Every line's margin rose, which rules out a price war and points to cheaper inputs; the energy margin's record 54.7% is borrowed, not earned. Stripping that windfall out of the trailing profit raises the growth the price needs from about 1.4% to about 1.8% at an 8% hurdle — still modest, but the safety margin is thinner than round 1 thought, and the third-quarter margin has already started to fade. The franchise is intact (dealers still prepay, up 43%, and the model needs almost no capital), so this is a maturing franchise priced for modest growth, not a broken business. Whether it is a pause or the end is still not answerable: it turns on fourth-quarter sales, the year-end dealer prepayment build, and whether the gross margin keeps sliding toward 45% while sales stay flat. Confidence: medium on the cost-windfall and mix reading (direct product lines), low on pause versus end.

**What would change our mind**
- Energy gross margin holds above ~52% even as input costs normalize: pricing power is real, not a windfall.
- Electrolyte and tea reaccelerate above ~30% with stable margin: the baton is passed, a pause.
- Q4 2026 revenue of 43 亿 or more with a year-end prepayment balance near 70 亿: a pause.
- Gross margin slides toward 45% while sales stay flat: the windfall is gone and mix is hurting — the end.

**Open questions**
- Volume versus price split for the flagship: is the 7% growth units or price? Needs the interim report or a channel check.
- What input costs (PET, sugar) drove the margin jump, and are they normalizing? Needs the interim report cost note.
- Is the electrolyte slowdown competition or category saturation? Needs competitor pricing and a channel check.
- Like-for-like regional growth, especially the home south market. Needs the interim report segment note.
- Q3-only product split is not in local data; the Q3 mechanism stays open.
